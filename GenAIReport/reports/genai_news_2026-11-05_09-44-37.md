## 🔬 Research Frontiers

### **[Perceptive Humanoid Motion from Simulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06908v1)**
This research addresses the critical data bottleneck in humanoid robot autonomy by leveraging simulation for perceptive motion, suggesting a promising path towards generalist humanoid robots capable of complex physical tasks. By bridging the sim-to-real gap, this work enables robots to interpret visual data for dynamic interaction in unstructured environments.

### **[Reinforcement Learning Enables Scaling Generalist Robot Policy Improvement](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06905v1)**
This paper explores how reinforcement learning can significantly enhance generalist robot policies, moving beyond zero-shot capabilities to continuous improvement from vast pre-training corpora. It points towards a future where robots can adapt and refine their skills across a wide array of tasks, accelerating the development of truly versatile robotic systems.

### **[BEAKER: An Expert-Curated Benchmark for Embodied Brains in Self-Driving Chemical Laboratories](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06911v1)**
BEAKER introduces a crucial benchmark for evaluating AI agents in self-driving chemical laboratories, pushing the boundaries of embodied automation in scientific discovery. This work is vital for developing AI that can perceive, reason, and act autonomously in complex experimental environments, accelerating chemical research and material science.

### **[FrontierPhysics: Evaluating Agents for End-to-End Physics Research](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06910v1)**
FrontierPhysics proposes a novel benchmark to assess AI agents' capabilities in end-to-end physics research, moving beyond isolated tasks to holistic scientific inquiry. This initiative is critical for fostering AI systems that can independently formulate hypotheses, design experiments, and interpret results, potentially revolutionizing the pace of scientific discovery.

### **[A Framework for Context Enrichment and Selective Robustness in LLM Social Agents](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06914v1)**
This research tackles the challenge of LLM agents' susceptibility to context manipulation in social simulations, proposing a framework for context enrichment and selective robustness. It's a significant step towards creating more reliable and ethically sound AI agents for social modeling, ensuring their actions are grounded in intended parameters rather than unintended biases or vulnerabilities.
