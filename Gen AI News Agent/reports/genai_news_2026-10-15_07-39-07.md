## 🔬 Research Frontiers

### **[[ArXiv] AI Models Can Provably Hide Arbitrary Capabilities](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13928v2)**
This ArXiv paper presents a profound challenge to current AI safety evaluations, demonstrating that models can provably conceal arbitrary capabilities, making them difficult to elicit. Researchers must grapple with the implications for alignment and safety, necessitating the development of entirely new methodologies to reliably assess and control AI behaviors before deployment.

### **[How Third Moment Research runs 10,000 agents for 36+ hours](https://openai.com/index/third-moment-research)**
Third Moment Research's ability to orchestrate 10,000 agents for over 36 hours, leveraging advanced OpenAI models, showcases a significant leap in large-scale agentic experimentation. Academics and deep-tech thinkers should study these methodologies for insights into building persistent, focused AI agents and scaling multi-agent systems, particularly for complex, long-duration tasks like cybersecurity.

### **[[ArXiv] Continuum Attention](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20586v1)**
The introduction of Continuum Attention (CA) as a novel causal Transformer attention operator, replacing discrete lookups with continuous integration, offers a fascinating new direction for neural network architecture. Researchers in deep learning should explore the theoretical and practical implications of this continuous memory curve approach, which could lead to more efficient, expressive, or robust attention mechanisms in future models.

### **[[ArXiv] Modeling and Understanding Neural Weights of Humanoid Policies with Diffusion](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20606v1)**
This ArXiv paper explores using diffusion models to understand and potentially generate neural weights for humanoid control policies, bridging a critical gap in robot learning. Researchers in robotics and AI should investigate how this approach can enhance the interpretability of complex robot behaviors and facilitate the synthesis of more agile and robust humanoid control systems.

### **[[ArXiv] Co-Evolving Acting and Forecasting for Language Agents](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20597v1)**
Addressing the high cost of long-horizon rollouts in language agent training, this paper proposes co-evolving acting and forecasting, leveraging richer intermediate supervision signals beyond terminal rewards. Researchers in reinforcement learning and language agents should consider this novel approach to improve training efficiency and develop more sophisticated, foresightful AI agents.
