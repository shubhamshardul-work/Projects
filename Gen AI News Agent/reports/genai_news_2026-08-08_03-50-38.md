### **[Learning When to Trust via Selective Context Preference Optimization](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06377v1)**
This paper addresses a critical challenge in LLM reliability: how models learn to discern trustworthy external information from misleading signals. The proposed "Selective Context Preference Optimization" offers a novel approach to improve model robustness and reduce susceptibility to adversarial inputs, pushing the boundaries of trustworthy AI. This research is vital for developing more reliable and secure AI systems that can operate effectively in complex, information-rich environments.

### **[The Bitter Lesson of Tool Calling](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06370v1)**
This research explores the complexities and potential pitfalls of LLMs using tools, particularly through programmatic tool calling, which extends capabilities beyond rigid JSON calls. While tool use significantly expands LLM functionality, this paper suggests it also introduces new challenges in control, predictability, and safety. It prompts a re-evaluation of current agentic AI design paradigms, urging researchers to consider the broader implications of giving LLMs more autonomy through external tools.

### **[An Optimal Agnostic PAC Algorithm](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06363v1)**
This theoretical paper presents an optimal agnostic PAC (Probably Approximately Correct) algorithm, marking a significant advancement in computational learning theory. By achieving a statistically optimal risk bound, it provides a foundational improvement for understanding and designing efficient learning algorithms. This work is particularly relevant for researchers in theoretical machine learning seeking to push the boundaries of what is provably learnable and how efficiently.

### **[AV-AIVAT: 74x Cheaper Agent Evaluation with Certified Anytime-Valid Stopping in Imperfect-Information Games](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06362v1)**
This paper introduces AV-AIVAT, a method for significantly reducing the cost of evaluating AI agents in imperfect-information games by providing certified anytime-valid stopping. This offers a more efficient and statistically sound approach to agent comparison, which has broad implications for research in reinforcement learning, game AI, and any domain where agent performance needs rigorous, yet cost-effective, assessment. It promises to accelerate research by making evaluation more accessible.
