## 🔬 Research Frontiers

### **[CodeMidas: Scaling Agentic Coding RL Environments from Code Itself](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22068v1)**
CodeMidas presents a novel approach to training capable coding agents by leveraging open-source codebases as a rich source of diverse tasks and reliable verifiers. This research is pivotal for advancing autonomous AI development, suggesting a path toward more robust and versatile coding agents that can learn directly from real-world code. The implications extend to automated software engineering, bug fixing, and even novel code generation.

### **[Value-Sensitive Delegation in Everyday AI Agent Use: Evidence from OpenClaw](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22067v1)**
This paper shifts the focus of AI agent evaluation from mere task completion to the critical dimension of user values, using Value Sensitive Design principles. Researchers should note this as a crucial step towards building more ethical and user-centric AI agents, emphasizing that successful delegation involves aligning with human priorities, not just efficiency. This work highlights the growing importance of human-AI interaction and trust in agentic systems.

### **[Benchmarking World Models for Continual Learning on Compositional Tasks](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22055v1)**
This research addresses a fundamental challenge for world models: the ability to learn continually across tasks without catastrophic forgetting. For deep-tech thinkers, this paper provides critical benchmarks and insights into how world models can adapt to new environments while retaining previously acquired knowledge. Solving continual learning is essential for building truly intelligent and adaptable AI systems that can operate in dynamic, real-world scenarios.

### **[BrainWideBench: Benchmarking large-scale pretraining and across-animal transfer in multi-region neural recordings](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22064v1)**
BrainWideBench explores the potential of large-scale neural recording data to inform and improve AI models, particularly through pretraining and cross-animal transfer. This interdisciplinary research bridges neuroscience and AI, offering new avenues for developing more biologically plausible and efficient learning algorithms. Researchers should consider how insights from distributed brain regions can inspire next-generation AI architectures and learning paradigms.

### **[Designer-RSI: Evolving Procedural Memory from User Traffic for Agentic Graphic Design](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22086v1)**
Designer-RSI delves into the complex task of agentic graphic design, focusing on evolving procedural memory from user traffic to create structured, editable artifacts. This work is significant for researchers in creative AI and agent systems, tackling the challenge of long-horizon tasks where programmatic oracles are absent. It suggests a future where AI agents can learn and adapt to human design preferences, leading to more intuitive and collaborative creative tools.
